# Complaint Investigation and Resolution — Example SOP

> **Educational simulation only.** Not a legal compliance procedure. Actual rules, deadlines, disclosures and escalation requirements must be determined by the applicable jurisdiction and organization.

## 1. Intake and registration
- Receive complaint from email, telephone, web or mail.
- Assign a unique case ID. Capture received date, channel, category, description and relevant records.
- **Do not record sensitive personal or financial information in this public demonstration.**

## 2. Triage and acknowledgement
- Categorize the issue and determine provisional impact and priority.
- Identify applicable client policies, reporting obligations, legal hold requirements and response timelines **before assigning any real deadline**.
- Assign case ownership and acknowledge receipt through the approved workflow.

## 3. Investigation and evidence review
- Review the consumer's allegation against the available account history, correspondence and business records.
- Interview responsible process owners when appropriate.
- Record which allegations are supported, disputed, or require further information.
- Maintain an audit trail of evidence sources, review dates and decisions.

## 4. Root-cause analysis
- Distinguish individual error from recurring control weakness.
- Group root causes as data-entry error, workflow gap, missing verification, processing delay, communication gap or unconfirmed.
- Consider whether similar complaints have occurred in other cases.

## 5. Corrective action and escalation
- Recommend appropriate record correction, communication remediation, process review or training.
- Escalate suspected systemic issues, potential legal violations, high-impact complaints or material control failures to designated compliance/legal personnel.
- Record action owner, target date, evidence of completion, and whether follow-up testing is required.

## 6. Resolution and closure
- Draft a clear, accurate response using approved templates and required review channels.
- Confirm corrective action and approvals before closing.
- Document response date, outcome, root cause and evidence of resolution.

## 7. Monitoring and reporting
- Review unresolved cases, overdue cases, recurring categories and repeated root causes.
- Trend complaint volumes and SLA exceptions; do not interpret fictional SLA targets as legal compliance.
- Summarize systemic observations and open actions for management review.
